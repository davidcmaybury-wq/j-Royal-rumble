# J! Royal Rumble — rules to paste

Each block below fits inside Discord's 2,000-character limit and can be posted
on its own. Post the short version as a pin, and the numbered sections as a

## THE SHORT VERSION (pin this)

**J! ROYAL RUMBLE — how it works**

Thirty players. Three start. One survives.

🔔 **Getting in.** Everybody draws an entry number. Three players start the
match; the rest enter one at a time as the game goes on. You'll get a countdown
on your buzzer telling you how close you are.

💰 **Scoring is the whole trick.** Answer correctly and **every other player in
the ring pays you the clue's value.** Four opponents on a $400 clue means you
gain $1,600 and they each drop $400. Everyone bleeds, constantly.

❌ **Miss and it hurts twice.** You lose the value and you're locked out of that
clue. It gets re-tossed to everyone else — and if somebody else takes it, you
pay them too.

🤐 **Nobody gets it?** Everyone in the ring loses half the value.

☠️ **Below zero and you're out.** Zero is still alive. Below is not.

🏆 **Last one standing wins.** That's the only result that counts. Everything
else — clues survived, toss outs, points drained, buzzer speed — is for
bragging rights and gets posted afterwards.

You'll need a phone or a second tab for your buzzer, and the game screen shared
on the call. Don't buzz early: jumping the lights costs you a quarter-second
lockout, and a quarter-second is a lifetime here.

## 1. THE SHAPE OF A MATCH

The board is six Jeopardy! categories, five clues each, $100 through $500 by
row. Some are pulled from the televised archive, some are written by our own
people — the host sets the mix. When a category is used up it's replaced by a
new one, so the board never runs dry.

Three players start in the ring. Everyone else waits in the queue in draw
order, entering one at a time on a fixed clue interval. The host sets that
interval, or lets it scale to the size of the field.

The match ends when the queue is empty and one player is left. That player
wins. There is no second place that means anything.

---

## 2. SCORING

**Correct** — you collect the clue's value from every other player in the ring.
Each of them loses that value; you gain the lot.

With five in the ring, a $500 clue swings $2,000 to you and $500 away from each
of the other four. With two of you left, the same clue is worth $500. **The
early game is where the big money is**, and that's deliberate — it gives the
players who entered first something to build with.

**Wrong** — you lose the value, and you're out of that clue. The others aren't
touched by your miss.

**The re-toss** — a missed clue goes straight back out to everyone still
eligible, as a fresh buzzer race. If somebody else converts it, you pay them
the value like everyone else. A miss followed by someone else's correct answer
costs you twice.

**Nobody gets it** — every player in the ring loses half the value. Passing is
not free.

---

## 3. ELIMINATION

Drop **below** zero and you're out. Exactly zero survives.

If one answer sinks several players at once, they all go together. The player
whose answer did it is credited with a **toss out** for each of them — the
closest thing this game has to throwing somebody over the top rope.

If a clue would somehow wipe the entire ring, the highest score survives.

Once you're out you stay out, but your buzzer keeps working in warm-up mode:
you can keep racing and see where you'd have placed against the live field.
Nothing you do counts, but it's better than watching.

---

## 4. THE CEILING

There's a maximum score, and **it falls as the match goes on.**

Anything above it gets clipped. It never drops below the amount a new entrant
walks in with, so nobody is capped on arrival.

This exists because of a problem in the format: without it, whoever draws last
walks into a field that's been grinding itself down for an hour and simply
outlasts them. The falling ceiling squeezes the survivors back toward the pack
so a late number is an advantage, not a guarantee.

---

## 5. CLEARING THE FIELD

Wipe out everyone in the ring while players are still queued and you've cleared
the field. You take a bonus equal to every clue left on the board, the board is
replaced entirely, and **two** fresh players come in.

It's rare. It should be.

---

## 6. BUZZING

The host reads the clue aloud, then arms the buzzers. Your buzzer lights up and
the clock starts. Fastest reaction takes it.

**Jump the lights and you're locked out for a quarter of a second.** The
penalty runs even if the buzzers go live while you're serving it — there's no
gain in guessing early.

Buzzer timing is compensated for the lag between the call audio and the app, so
everyone is racing from the same moment. Your reaction time is measured on your
own device, to a tenth of a millisecond.

One buzz per player per race.

---

## 7. IF YOUR CONNECTION DROPS

You keep bleeding. Clues carry on, your score keeps falling, and you can be
eliminated while you're offline. Harsh, but the alternative is that dropping
out becomes a tactic when you're about to get drained.

Reconnecting puts you back exactly where you were — same score, same entry
number, same place in the queue. Just get back in.

---

## 8. WHAT GETS TRACKED

Posted at the end, sortable, and shareable:

- **Clues survived** — how long you lasted, from entry to elimination
- **Toss outs** — players you personally sank
- **Drained** — total points you took off other people
- **Peak** — your highest score
- **Correct / missed**
- **Buzzer attempts, races won, win rate**
- **Average and best reaction time**, and the single fastest buzz of the match

The winner is the winner. Everything else is an argument for the group chat.

## HOW TO JOIN (paste this before a match)

1. Open the buzzer link on your phone or a second tab.
2. Type in the four-letter room code the host reads out.
3. Add your name, and a photo if you want one next to it.
4. **Leave that page open.** It's your buzzer for the whole match.
5. Watch the shared screen for the clues.

Sound helps — you'll hear a horn when you enter and a countdown in the last
three clues before your number comes up. Tap the screen once when you join so
your browser allows it.
